About the Ban

Byte Dance, TikTok’s parent company, along with all other private companies in China, are required by law to cooperate with intelligence gathering.

For the last few years, Congress has demanded the sale of TikTok to a U.S.-based company or the social media app would be banned in the United States. With the controversy surrounding the TikTok ban, it is important to recognize the real national security threat the app poses to the American government and people.

TikTok, the social media platform that has taken the U.S. by storm, was first investigated in 2019 by the Committee on Foreign Investment in the United States due to national security risks. In 2020, President Donald Trump issued an executive order to ban TikTok which is inevitably blocked by injunctions. Now after four years of negotiations, and the app actually being banned for 24-hours, the new divest or ban date is approaching. If an agreement is not met, Americans will lose TikTok.

Here is why this needs to happen:

TikTok has far more access to our personal data than we realize. Oftentimes when users hear TikTok collects their data, they don’t even bat an eye. People often say, “Why would China care that I watched an AI cat video for the 100th time?” This may be true. However, TikTok knows much more about you than just your preferences. On the University of Ottawa website, an article entitled, “TikTok use and privacy risks” shares a list of data has on each of its users including; email addresses, phone numbers, uploaded content and information mobile carrier, wireless connections, device brand and model, operating system, browsing history, ways of consuming data, time spent watching posts, searches, apps, filenames and filetypes and location age, image, personal contacts, relationship status, preferences, and access to camera roll– to name a few. I encourage you all to really think about the implications of China having all of that data on half the American population

ByteDance is required by law to deliver American data to the Chinese government. Although the government is concerned about the technology security of civilians, the point was made in The U.S. Supreme Court’s decision, TikTok Inc. v. Garland revealed that China can also track the locations of Federal employees and contractors. According to FBI Director Christopher Wray this will enable TikTok to collect personal information for blackmail, and conduct corporate espionage. The great fear is China will essentially become a puppetmaster in American politics and economics.

At a time when children are more online, more impressionable, and more exposed than ever, the question becomes simple: if a platform cannot guarantee the protection of your family’s information, is it worth the risk?
